**D-003 | 2026-07-31 | Topic: nuisance degrees of freedom in LLM event-driven trading pipelines.**
The portfolio's standing thesis is that agent outcomes are a property of the *harness*, not the
model. ICAIF is the venue where that thesis has an established, high-status statistical counterpart:

LLM trading result is manufactured by pipeline choices that no paper reports and no theory prefers.
*Why this and not an SE topic:* ICAIF reviewers are finance-and-AI; a software-engineering agent
paper would be off-scope. This framing is on-scope, uses the portfolio's real comparative advantage
(measurement rigour, free local inference, retrospective replay of public data), and is constructive
rather than purely diagnostic, which is the portfolio's recurring reviewer complaint
(`IDEA_BANK.md` Part 1).

## Amendments to the frozen pre-registration

**A-1 | 2026-07-31 | `xrank` groups by ENTRY SESSION, not by filing date.**
`notes/preregistration.md` section 3 wrote "cross-sectional rank within the filing date". That
grouping is not tradable: a filing accepted at 20:00 ET and one accepted at 08:00 ET share a filing
date, but the first is not knowable when the second is traded at 09:30 that morning. Ranking within
the entry session is the tradable version of the same idea and is what the code implements. This is
a correctness fix, not a result-driven change; it was made before any Sharpe ratio existed. Declared
amendment count so far: **1**.

**A-2 | 2026-07-31 | Events are additionally required to leave room for the longest holding period
under both entry rules** (`t0 + 5 < n_sessions`). Without it the event set would shrink as `hold`
grows, so `hold` would move the sample size as well as the strategy, and the variance decomposition
would attribute a sample-size effect to a design factor. Declared amendment count: **2**.

**D-015 | 2026-07-31 | REVERSES D-010: the model factor IS fully crossed with the LLM-side grid.**
A throughput benchmark on the actual rig (one consumer GPU Laptop, 16-way concurrency) measured 2 to 4
Ollama calls per second at the truncation lengths in the grid, which makes the fully crossed
3 x 3 x 2 = 18 passes over 6,350 events roughly a 10-hour background job rather than an infeasible
one. A balanced design makes the variance decomposition a clean sum-of-squares partition instead of
an unbalanced approximation, and that decomposition is the paper's primary claim, so the compute is
worth spending. Work is chunked by event with all three models completing each chunk, so an
interrupted run still leaves a balanced design.

**A-3 | 2026-07-31 | The event sample is a pre-declared random subsample of 3,000 of the 6,350
8-K filings, and generation is capped at 64 tokens.**
The pre-registration froze the grid before the throughput of the actual rig was known. Measured on
the real prompts: `qwen2.5:7b-instruct` sustains about 2 calls/s, but `mistral:7b-instruct-v0.2` and
`phi3:mini` answer the probability prompt in prose rather than with a bare number, so they generate
roughly 45 tokens per call and sustain under 1 call/s. The full 6,350-event factorial is about 27
GPU-hours, which does not fit. Two changes, both made before any Sharpe ratio existed:
  * the event set is the first 3,000 of a seed-fixed shuffle (`data/event_sample.json`), which is a
    uniform random subsample and keeps the design perfectly balanced;
  * `num_predict = 64`, chosen because a 32-token cap truncated verbose models before they reached
    a number, which would have manufactured non-compliance as an artefact of the harness rather
    than measuring it. At 64 tokens, 70/72 and 69/72 sampled answers from the two verbose models
    contain a digit.
The statistic whose precision matters is a Sharpe ratio over 243 daily observations, and the number
of sessions is unchanged; the subsample thins the daily cross-section from about 26 to about 12
events, which is disclosed. Preferring a balanced complete factorial over more events is a design
call: the paper's primary claim is a variance decomposition, and an unbalanced design would make
that decomposition an approximation. Declared amendment count: **3**.

**D-016 | 2026-07-31 | One filing is one event: duplicate accessions from dual-class tickers are
collapsed, keeping the alphabetically first ticker.** The S&P 500 list contains three CIKs with two
share classes each (GOOG/GOOGL, FOXA/FOX, NWSA/NWS), so 274 of the 6,350 filing rows are the same
8-K reaching the pipeline twice. Left alone, one piece of news would open two positions and those
names would carry double weight in every configuration. The rule is fixed and applied identically
everywhere, so it cannot generate between-configuration dispersion. Unique events: **6,076**.
The data gauntlet now asserts that every duplicated accession is explained by a dual-class CIK, and
that assertion was validated by injecting a duplicate that is not (it fires).

**D-017 | 2026-07-31 | Design review by `a commercial reviewing model` through `a review wrapper` (identity gate
passed; report at `notes/agy_design_r1.md`). Findings acted on:**
* *Confirmed correct by independent reading:* the tradability boundary, the cross-sectional ranking,
  the expanding per-ticker demeaning, both entry rules' daily return series, the gross-exposure
  normalisation, the market-neutral variant, and the placement of the 5 bp costs. Also confirmed
  that applying the SAME permutation to all 18 score vectors is required, not optional, because
  independent permutations would destroy the cross-configuration correlation and overstate the null.
* *Acted on, objection 1 (the sharpest):* "a one-year Sharpe is noisy enough to produce the spread
  by itself". Added a PAIRED stationary bootstrap: sessions are resampled once per replication and
  every configuration is re-evaluated on the same resampled sessions, so the common sampling error
  cancels and what remains is the sampling error of the between-configuration differences.
  Reported as within-configuration sd against between-configuration spread.
* *Acted on, objection 2:* the participation ratio is a spectral heuristic, not a theorem about the
  maximum of a correlated Gaussian vector. The paper now reports equation (1) evaluated at every
  candidate `N` against the measured permutation maximum, and reports the `N` that reproduces it,
  rather than asserting that `N_eff` is the right one. The caveat is stated in the released tool.
* *Acted on, objection 3:* "the ranking was manufactured by the widest levels". Added a trimmed-grid
  re-analysis that drops the most aggressive level of the two factors a reviewer would name
  (threshold 0.20 and the five-day hold) and reports the shares again.
* *Acted on, additional:* the variance partition is now also reported for mean daily return and for
  daily volatility separately, so the paper can say whether the pipeline factors move the numerator
  or merely rescale leverage.
* *Disclosed rather than fixed:* multi-day holds are modelled at constant target weight without
  intra-hold drift or the rebalancing cost that implies; adjusted prices are back-adjusted total
  return series; sessions with a single event cannot be permuted, so the null is conservative to
  that extent (the fraction of permutable events is reported).
* *Rejected:* fitting a generalised extreme value distribution to the null maximum. The exact
  permutation distribution is already in hand, so fitting a parametric family to it adds an
  assumption without adding information.

**A-4 | 2026-07-31 | The lenient parser's integer regex is corrected, and out-of-range integers are
skipped rather than clipped.** The pre-registration says "first number anywhere". The shipped
implementation of that had three defects, all found by inspecting real model output before any
Sharpe ratio existed:
  * `\d{1,3}` with no boundaries matched a FRAGMENT of a longer number, so "2026" yielded 202;
  * it matched the document type named in our own prompt, so every answer containing "Form 8-K"
    scored 0.08;
  * clipping to [0,100] turned "S&P 500" into a maximal bullish score, which is not leniency but a
    bug that manufactures a signal out of boilerplate.
The parser now takes the first integer that is genuinely in [0,100], skipping out-of-range ones,
and refuses fragments and the "8-K" token. It is still a naive parser, which is the point of the
factor; it is no longer a broken one. Unit tests for all six cases are in the validation run.
A separate fix: the P2 confidence is now read from after the label, with an explicit `None` check
rather than `or`, because a confidence of exactly 0 is falsy. Declared amendment count: **4**.

**D-022 | 2026-07-31 | The title's "harness, not the model" framing is an OVERCLAIM and will be
replaced.** Reading the drafted results against the preview data exposed a conflict between two
measures that the paper reports side by side:
  * the pre-registered variance decomposition gives the seven post-LLM factors 13.4% of the Sharpe
    variance against 2.1% for the model, a ratio of about six, and H2 passes on that test;
  * the like-for-like swap gives 0.72 for changing the model with everything else held fixed
    against 0.84 for re-deciding the pipeline around a fixed model, a ratio of 1.2.
They disagree because the model factor acts almost entirely through an interaction: a strict parser
rejects every answer from some model-prompt cells, so swapping the model there switches the
configuration between trading and not trading. The main-effect share is small precisely because the
effect is conditional, and interactions carry over 80% of the variance.
Putting the ranking in the TITLE would therefore be selective reporting of the more flattering of
two measures we ourselves computed. The pre-registered hypothesis is still reported, with both
measures and the mechanism, in the results.
The claim that survives both measures untouched, and is the strongest thing in the paper, needs no
model comparison at all: with the model, the prompt and the truncation ALL FIXED, the 432 post-LLM
configurations alone span an average of 3.8 in annualised Sharpe. The title and the abstract will
be rebuilt around that and around the permutation null. Final wording is chosen once the full run
lands; the directional gates in `paper/consistency.py` hold the rest of the prose to the data.

**D-023 | 2026-08-01 | FINAL RESULTS on the complete run (54,000 LLM calls, 0 errors; 2,978
tradable events; 250 sessions; 7,776 configurations; 1,000 permutation replications).**
Recorded here in full so that the write-up can be audited against them.
  * Sharpe from **-3.85 to 2.46**, sd **1.01**, IQR 1.22, median -0.24. 28.8% profitable, 9.2% above
    Sharpe 1, and 14.8% never trade at all.
  * With model, prompt and truncation ALL FIXED, the 432 post-LLM configurations span **4.89** on
    average (up to 6.31), sd 0.94.
  * H1 holds (sd 1.01 >= 0.5). H3 holds (N_eff 20 vs 7,776). H5 holds. Falsification NOT triggered.
  * H2 holds but **narrowly**: post-LLM 8.7% vs model 7.0%. The model is the LARGEST SINGLE main
    effect (range 0.59, ahead of signal at 0.50), and 83.3% of the variance is interaction. The
    like-for-like swap gives 0.87 (model) against 0.97 (pipeline), a ratio of 1.1. The paper reports
    all three and draws the honest conclusion: no family of choices is the culprit, the interaction
    is, and Table 3 gives its mechanism.
  * **Compliance is the mechanism, and it is striking.** Each model answers a DIFFERENT one of the
    three prompts in the requested format: Qwen2.5 100/100/100, Mistral 0/0/74, Phi-3 0/92/4. Under
    a strict parser, "which model" and "how strictly do you parse" are one question.
  * **Permutation null: the signal-free pipeline WINS.** Best-of-grid Sharpe 2.72 (sd 0.40) against
    2.46 for the real pipeline, p = 0.746. The modal literature configuration gives p > 0.99. On
    this sample these three models contribute nothing detectable.
  * Deflated Sharpe of the best configuration: 0.995 at N=1, 0.713 at N_eff, 0.335 at the Li-Ji
    count, 0.072 at the nominal grid size. Equation (1) predicts 1.93 / 2.86 / 3.84 against a
    measured 2.72, so **the participation ratio under-corrects**; the trial count that reproduces
    the measurement is 160. The protocol section now says so instead of recommending N_eff alone.
  * Split-half: rank correlation 0.15; the first-half best falls from 3.73 to 0.53, still the 72nd
    percentile of the grid. The preview's much more dramatic version (2nd percentile) did NOT
    survive the full sample, and the drafted sentence claiming selection is worse than no selection
    was caught by the directional gate and rewritten.
  * Entity-masked arm: scores correlate 0.68 with the unmasked ones and shift 0.054 on average, yet
    the post-LLM grid's median, maximum and spread are all within 0.12 of the unmasked arm.
    Contamination through issuer recognition is not what produces the dispersion.

The finding that matters most is one no free round caught, because the free rounds were checking
numbers and this one checked *inference*:
  * **The permutation does not identify the null the paper claimed.** Permuting scores within a
    session enforces within-session exchangeability. It leaves session-level and market-timing

    exact one. Every sentence resting on "the language model contributes nothing", including the
    abstract's "The alpha is in the grid, not in the model", has been narrowed to what the benchmark
